What changed in Y14.5-2018

The changes from the 2009 edition that matter for these lessons and for reading drawings on the floor. This page is built from secondary summaries. It is not a complete change list and doesn't replace reading the 2018 standard.

Before you cite anything: every section in 2018 was renumbered, though the order of topics stayed the same. A 2009 section number is not a 2018 section number. Section numbers on this page come from secondary sources and haven't been checked against the standard itself.

The quick swap list

Confirmed changes

Source-confirmed from secondary summaries (Mitutoyo, Made to Measure, GD&T Basics, FARO). Fine to teach; the section numbers are still to be checked.

Concentricity and symmetry removed source-confirmed

What changed

Both are gone in 2018: the terms, the symbols and the concept. They were median-point controls, which made them costly to inspect.

What it means for us

Lessons, problems and the stack workbook use position, runout or profile instead. The site's Problem creator already labels conc/sym as "2009 only", and the error checks flag them. On an older drawing, read them as 2009 controls and ask whether the revision can move to position or runout.

Verified reference §14.1, §7.2, §7.3 · Mitutoyo slides cite appendix A-8.4 (secondary)

Dynamic profile modifier added source-confirmed

What changed

A small triangle after the profile tolerance lets profile refine form without also controlling size.

What it means for us

It's 2018-only, so don't expect it on 2009 drawings. It's rarely needed: on a cylinder, cylindricity already refines form. Mentioned in the profile lesson, not modeled there.

Verified reference §14.2 · Made to Measure cites ¶11.10–11.11 (secondary)

Every section renumbered source-confirmed

What changed

The order of topics is unchanged, but all sections have new numbers. The book grew from about 214 to about 326 pages.

What it means for us

Never quote a 2009 section number as a 2018 number. For example, pattern location (composite and multiple single-segment) is cited as 2018 §10.5, per secondary sources.

Verified reference §14.3 · Made to Measure, GD&T Basics, FARO (secondary)

Nominal isn't a target source-confirmed

What changed

A new statement in the general section: the as-designed (nominal) value doesn't set a functional or manufacturing target.

What it means for us

It backs up how the stack tools work: unequal tolerances become a mean ± equal, and the middle of the limits is no "worse" than the printed nominal. At the machine, aim where the process and the function want, not at the nominal by default.

Verified reference §14.4 · Mitutoyo cites 4.1(q) (secondary)

CF also covers continuous surfaces source-confirmed

What changed

The CF (continuous feature) symbol is clarified to apply to continuous surfaces too, not just interrupted features of size.

What it means for us

Minor. It only matters if a lesson or drawing shows CF.

Verified reference §14.5 · GD&T Basics (secondary)

Otherwise, no major conceptual changes source-confirmed

What changed

One summary's overall view: beyond the items above, 2018 reorganized and clarified more than it changed concepts.

What it means for us

2009-based references stay usable for concepts, with a 2018 note wherever a topic changed. It's still one source's opinion, and the change list isn't complete.

Unconfirmed: don't teach these yet

"Each radial element" shown as profile of a line provisional

Reported change

The 2009 orientation note "EACH RADIAL ELEMENT" is reportedly shown in 2018 as profile of a line to a datum axis.

Why it would matter

It affects how radial line elements are taught ("each element" controls).

Verified reference §14.6 · repost of a GD&T Basics article only

Datum wording and rocking datum features provisional

Reported change

New datum wording (e.g. a "true geometric counterpart" term) and one default stabilization rule for a primary datum feature that rocks.

Why it would matter

It could change part of the datum reference frame lesson, where a primary datum feature isn't flat.
